## Problem

**Head:** You've heard the stories. Maybe you've lived one.
**Lede:** The bid is never the expensive part. The expensive part is everything that happens after a contractor gets your deposit.
1. **The disappearing act.** Calls go to voicemail. The crew shows up Tuesday, vanishes until next week, and your opening date, move-in, or lease-up slides with it.
2. **The change-order spiral.** A low number wins the job, then "unforeseen conditions" turn it into a high one.
3. **Five subs, zero owners.** The electrician blames the framer. You end up project-managing your own project.

**Turn:** We built this company around the opposite of all three.

## Proof

The current proof is honest and factual: the 1991 founding, years in business, the one-contract model, and written commitments ("We put it in writing"). **Real reviews are the biggest missing conversion lever.** Collect them using the prompts below.

## Testimonial suggestions (prompt 8)

**Don't publish invented testimonials.** The FTC's 2024 rule on fake reviews makes them a legal risk, and they kill trust once someone spots them. Instead, send recent clients these questions. Each one pulls out a specific, believable quote:

1. "What were you worried about before you hired us, and did that happen?"
2. "Did the project finish on the date we gave you? What did that mean for you?"
3. "Was the final invoice what you expected from the bid?"
4. "What would you tell another property manager / business owner / homeowner who's on the fence?"

Aim for one testimonial per audience: a commercial tenant or owner, an apartment manager, and a homeowner. Each one should name the person, their role, the city, and the project type. Next to the testimonials, show Google review count and stars once you have 10 or more.
